Abstract

Humanoid robots require jogging interfaces that go beyond conventional single-arm joint or end-effector increments, because on-site adjustment often involves coupled arms, torso participation, task-specific invariants, and nearby geometric limits. This paper designs semantic jog modes for humanoid upper-body teaching. Each mode defines the operator command, the task quantity to be preserved, and the waist, joint-limit, and posture constraints active during human-guided robot adjustment. The resulting mode contracts include single-arm, bimanual absolute-pose, relative-distance, twisting, and waist-coupled adjustment primitives, which are converted into a common residual-and-constraint model for constrained local updates. To keep these modes usable near kinematic and waist-corridor boundaries, we compute dual-quaternion task manipulability in the extracted geometric twist space and introduce an availability weighting based on vector field inequalities. The weighted manipulability is used as a secondary posture-quality bias, while the commanded jog motion and preserved invariants remain primary objectives. Hardware experiments on a humanoid robot demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method for semantic jog execution, constraint handling, boundary-aware posture adjustment, and representative manipulation tasks.
