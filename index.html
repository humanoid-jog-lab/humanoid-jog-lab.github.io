Abstract

This paper studies humanoid upper-body cooperative jog teaching from the viewpoint of constraint-aware manipulability and semantic-preserving local control. During repeated operator-specified increments, the robot must move the commanded cooperative quantity while preserving mode-dependent invariants and respecting local geometric feasibility. We formalize each jogging mode as a mode contract that specifies the commanded variable, the preserved cooperative quantity, the active joint set, and the vector-field-inequality rows defining feasible local motion. To evaluate dexterity in over-parameterized cooperative task spaces, we introduce a twist-extracted manipulability measure and a vector-field-inequality-aware extension that attenuates motion directions approaching joint-limit and waist-corridor boundaries. The resulting manipulability signal is then used as a low-priority posture-quality objective in a safeguarded local update, where commanded and preserved quantities remain primary. Simulations and hardware demonstrations validate the resulting semantic preservation, constraint handling, and boundary-aware manipulability behavior.
